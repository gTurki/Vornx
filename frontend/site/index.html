VORNX · The Difference

What makes VORNX different?

Not just an access card, but a smart layer that connects identity to the access decision.

A traditional access card

  • Access is usually tied to the system that issued the credential.
  • Permission management may be spread across multiple systems.
  • Changing access may require separate steps in each system.
  • Event records may be scattered across different platforms.

The VORNX vision

  • One identity that aims to link access experiences across compatible environments.
  • Connected management of a person’s identity and permissions.
  • Permissions updated under policy, without necessarily replacing the card.
  • A more connected view of access events, depending on integration capabilities.
Illustrative scenario

One day. One card. Multiple access experiences.

  1. 01 — WorkplaceThe employee presents the card at the facility entrance, and the credential and entry permission are verified under the compatible system’s policies.
  2. 02 — ParkingThe same card is used at the parking facility, provided the parking system supports the required integration and the permission is active.
  3. 03 — GymIt is then used at the gym, where entry permission follows membership status and the verification result is applied under the gym’s rules and the available integration.
  4. 04 — EventsAt events, the same card carries access scoped to a specific time and place, with no need for a separate card or system.
VORNX · The Card

The VORNX Card

One identity. One access point into a complete system.

A smart card that presents a person’s identity and credential. It is the point of interaction; verification, policy, and entry are handled by the connected systems.

The role of the card

  • Presenting identity and credentialThe card presents credentials to compatible readers.
  • Interacting with access systemsIt interacts with compatible readers and systems under supported credential protocols and integration methods.
  • Connecting to a wider systemIt is part of a vision that coordinates identity, permissions, and the access experience across multiple environments.

Where does the card’s role end?

On its own, the card is not a permission management system, does not decide by itself to allow entry, and does not operate doors or services directly. These functions depend on verification and control systems, access policies, and approved integration methods.

VORNX · The Platform

The platform behind the card

Identity, permissions, and access decisions within one system.

The VORNX platform coordinates identity, credentials, and permissions to evaluate access requests against policy and context. Readers, locks, and entry stay with the organization’s systems.

Designed to integrate, not to replace the organization’s systems

Readers, locks, control devices, and the actual enforcement of entry remain within the organization’s systems and security controls. VORNX does not directly control doors.

Contextual intelligence

The platform is designed to use request context and risk indicators to support more appropriate access decisions; approved policies remain the basis of every decision.

    VORNX / Security & Governance

    Security is part of the system’s design.

    Security and governance guide how VORNX is designed: separation of responsibilities, controlled authorization, and reviewability.

    Security principles

    Server-side authorizationThe authorization path is designed so access decisions are processed on the server, not by relying on interface logic alone.
    Policy-based controlAccess requests are evaluated against approved policies and permissions within the integration scope.
    Least privilegePermissions are limited to what is needed for the authorized function.
    Separation between organizationsControls designed to keep each organization’s data and permissions isolated from other organizations.
    Audit trailsSupport for recording sensitive operations and reviewing them, depending on platform and integration capabilities.
    Safe handling when verification failsAuthorization paths are designed so access is not allowed when the request cannot be verified, under the approved integration method.

    Security responsibility boundaries

    VORNX responsibility

    • Authorization controls the platform implements within its scope.
    • Protection of the event records the platform manages.
    • Security of the connections and integration interfaces within its responsibility.

    Integrated system responsibility

    • Security of its readers, devices, and operating infrastructure.
    • Allowing or denying entry inside the environment under the integration method.
    • Its own security policies, controls, and administrative permissions.

    Security controls and integration mechanisms are subject to design, implementation, and testing. No security certifications or accreditations will be shown until they are obtained and their scope is verified.
